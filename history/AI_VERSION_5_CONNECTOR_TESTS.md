## What to print

Start with the `ai_version_5_style_connector_suite/3dpr.stl` under `parts/`.
It contains nine separate pieces on one A1 mini sized layout:

| Position, viewed from above (+Z) | Trial | Pieces | Identification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upper left | A: single cantilever latch | Plate + rail | One edge notch |
| Upper right | B: paired cantilever latches | Plate + rail | Two edge notches |
| Lower left | C: captive fork key | Plate + rail + loose key | Three edge notches |
| Lower right | D: existing countersunk screw joint | Plate + rail | No notches |

Each design also has its own population record and folder: `snap_single`,
`snap_twin`, `snap_key`, `screw_reference`. Its `3dpr.stl` is the smaller kit.
Individual `plate.stl`, `rail.stl` and C's `key.stl` permit inexpensive reprints.
`assembled.stl` is a visual reference only. Do not print it as a fused assembly.
The print positions are fixed in source; removing parts loses that layout,
so use the notches to keep each rail with its corresponding receiver.

All rails use the current **11 × 10 mm** end section, **32 mm** grip length,
and **7.5 mm** receiver thickness. Receiver coupons are **15 × 14 mm**.
These are local joint tests, not long-rail bending or full cabinet tests.
The container field stays in population for taxonomy/context; no container
is included or fitted in these samples.

## The designs

**A — single latch.** One 1.6 mm thick arm, rooted 8 mm into the rail.
One hook carries withdrawal load; the asymmetry may make it easier to peel
or twist loose. A rigid central tongue handles alignment independently.

**B — twin latches.** Two 1.4 mm arms, rooted 12 mm into the rail.
Both must recover beyond the plate. This compares more balanced retention
and longer springs with A's shorter, thicker single spring.

Both direct latches have 1.1 mm inside root fillets, 4 mm arm height, a
0.65 mm hook projection and a 2.4 mm insertion ramp. With 0.25 mm per-side
socket clearance, each hook nominally deflects 0.40 mm while entering.
Flat retaining shoulders sit 0.2 mm outside the receiver face when seated.
The hook tips project 2.6 mm outside that face: these trials are deliberately
not flush. The blind guide engages 3 mm. Socket entrances flare at the
inside face; the outer retaining edge stays square.

**C — separate captive key.** A rigid tongue passes through the receiver.
Insert the small fork sideways through its exposed slot. Two 0.8 mm arms
on the key flex inward and recover beyond the tongue, keeping the key
captive. The cross-key then blocks withdrawal of the rigid rail tongue.
The key has 0.4 mm inside root fillets. This keeps the spring replaceable,
but adds a small loose part and a roughly 3.3 mm bridge in the rail slot.
Its two-line spring sections are the most sensitive to extrusion settings.
It also protrudes beyond the outside face; check access and appearance
before considering it for a full unit.

**D — screw reference.** The existing v4 pointed key, M3 × 16 countersunk
screw and side-loaded M3 nut are generated by the existing drawing routine.
Use one screw and one nut for this single-ended test. The short rail also
retains the unused nut pocket at its other end. This is the comparison
joint for stiffness, assembly effort and pull-out behavior.

The snap receivers are experimental matching coupons, not drop-in replacements
for existing v4 plates. No full cabinet interface has been changed.

## Print and test consistently

1. Use the intended PETG, dry and printed with your established temperature
   profile. Start with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, four walls and 100%
   infill for these small comparison coupons. The 0.8 mm key arms will have
   fewer actual lines; check that the slicer preserves them.
2. Keep the supplied orientation. Springs bend in the bed plane. Plates sit
   outside-face-down; rails and the key sit on their long flat faces.
   A/B and the loose key need no internal supports. Inspect C's short slot
   bridge and D's existing hardware bridges. Keep brims out of spring gaps.
3. Remove strings and any first-layer flare, without filing the hook shoulders
   to force a fit. Record cleanup. First-layer compensation is a slicer choice;
   all CAD clearances remain at one default, adjustable in population.
4. A/B: push until the rail end seats against the plate. Inspect the far side
   and confirm each hook has sprung back. A snug sliding fit is not proof of
   locking. C: seat the tongue, then push in the fork until both hooks recover.
5. Compare hand assembly effort, end play, side play and visible whitening
   with D. Record failures as well as successful assemblies. Test three
   specimens of each candidate before drawing conclusions.
6. Hold the receiver by its outer border and the rail by its solid rear grip.
   Pull along the rail axis with a force gauge or scale if available, using
   the same fixture and rate for each design. Record measured insertion and
   pull-out force, failure location and whether the lock disengaged or broke.
   Stop comparative non-destructive tests at the intended service load;
   use separate specimens for destructive pull tests.
7. Apply the same chosen sustained load to each design for 24 hours, then
   inspect slip, arm recovery and loosening. Repeat at the actual intended
   service temperature before choosing a joint. No safe load is established
   by these drawings. Record the chosen load, time and temperature.

Use `connector_tests/results_template.csv` as a blank recording sheet.
Copy it for each run; generation never overwrites completed results.
These are mainly permanent locks: do not use repeated forced pull-apart cycles
as the only qualification. Inspect a sacrificial section or use fresh specimens.

## Adjustable source and checks

`get_ai_version_5_defaults()` and `get_ai_version_5_options()` in population
own the variants, dimensions and material intent. There are five records,
all `connector_test`; none is a cabinet. `connector_clearance` remains one
settable value, 0.25 mm per side, without clearance taxonomy variants.

Main drawing provides the solid coupon bodies. All receiver openings, spring
reliefs, guide tongues, filleted hooks, key slots and identifying notches live
in `get_container_cutout_ai_version_5()` as explicit multiline dictionaries.
Exported files and instructions are generated by `scad_help`.

Run `python action_validate_ai_version_5.py` for the complete
populate → OOMP → SCAD pipeline, STL exports, previews and checks.
The validator redraws v5 only via `scad_filter="ai_version_5"`; population
and OOMP still run first. Existing source-to-CAD release checks protect older
versions. Normal unfiltered build commands continue to redraw everything.
It checks positive watertight pieces, bed placement, exact kit quantities,
seated fit, retaining interference on withdrawal, guide engagement and sampled
insertion paths with prescribed arm deflection. The deformation is a geometric
clearance test, **not FEA or a force prediction**. The rectangular-cantilever
strain estimate is only a screening number; it is not a PETG allowable strain.
Physical test results decide which, if any, should progress.

Current verification: **19 exported views, four joint checks, 43 geometry
unit tests and seven comparison tests pass**. The suite occupies approximately
93 × 104 mm and contains nine closed printable pieces. Its solid-CAD PETG
mass estimate is about 23 g; actual slicing will differ. The full report is
`validation_ai_version_5/report.json`. No physical results are recorded yet.

The validator checks all three existing release locks and the v4 input/CAD
baseline captured before this work. It must not rewrite that baseline to
hide a regression. Comparison treats these as hidden samples, includes no pots,
excludes reference hardware cost and assumes PETG density 1.27 g/cm³ for CAD
mass only. The existing GBP 0.01/g filament rate is an estimate, not a quote.
For connector-only changes, `python action_prepare_drawer_comparison.py
--scad-filter ai_version_5` runs the same pipeline before and after refreshing
all stale CAD measurements. Omit the filter when other drawing styles change.

## Design references

The choice to orient the spring in the bed plane and let it recover after
engagement follows the general FDM and cantilever guidance in
[Formlabs' snap-fit guide](https://formlabs.com/eu/blog/designing-3d-printed-snap-fit-enclosures/).
Print orientation, tolerances and material choice also need to match the
process, as discussed in
[Prusa's modeling guidance](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135).
Neither reference validates these particular dimensions or a load rating.
