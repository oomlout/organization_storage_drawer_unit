# Version 4: modular drawer units

Developed 2026-10-20. Version 3 is preserved in `releases/ai_version_3/`;
versions 1 and 2 remain frozen. Development now belongs in version 4.

## Parts and assembly

Each unit uses a **front plate with a lip and a rear plate without one**, full-depth removable rails and
M3 x 16 countersunk screws with M3 nuts. The same regular rail is used for
all drawer-bearing shelves, the top and the top corners. Only the bottom
row uses a separate, thicker rail. Rails are shared across all grid sizes
using the same container depth: 95.5 mm for Systempak 209 / 100 ml, and
94.5 mm for Systempak 214 / 550 ml. Do not mix these two rail lengths.

Both plates retain the stepped container opening and matching rail mounts.
Only the front plate has the 6 mm catch and its reverse ramp. The rear has
no projecting catch or catch ramp, leaving its opening clear. This supersedes
the earlier identical-plate design at the user's request. Flip the rear plate
about the vertical Y axis for assembly, keeping its keys inward. Check actual
tray seating in a 1 x 1 frame before printing a large set.

Each plate has a 3 mm projecting, pointed alignment key around every screw.
The rail end receives it with 0.25 mm side clearance and 0.2 mm end clearance.
The pointed socket roof is shaped for a rail printed flat. Each M3 x 16
countersunk screw enters from the outside of the plate. An OOBB nut pocket
in the rail receives the nut sideways: `overhang=True`, `clearance="left"`.
In the current library, **left opens towards local +X**; `right` opens -X.
`nut_side_clearance` is adjustable in population, without separate taxonomy.
Load both nuts before closing the unit with its second plate.

The plate is 7.5 mm thick. Nut pockets are 5.5–8.5 mm inward from each rail
end, so their far face lies 16 mm from the plate's outside face. Nut pockets
are 3 mm deep; the CAD insertion test uses a nominal 5.5 mm across-flats,
2.4 mm thick M3 hex nut. Actual nuts and printer tolerances still need a fit
trial. OOBB overhang reliefs are present; the horizontal screw bores and
side-entry slot ceilings still require short bridging in the slicer.

Regular rails have a 4 mm running section and 10 mm reinforced ends; bottom
rails have a 6 mm running section. End reinforcement is 12 mm long with
7.2 mm transitions. Rails print on their long flat faces, approximately
11 x 95.5 mm on the bed and 10 mm high, rather than standing upright.

The outside border is 10.5 mm to accept a regular rail at the top corners.
12 mm breakaway ears preserve the 2 mm bed margin on the existing A1 mini,
P1P and 400 mm maximum grids. These v4 plates are larger than the v3 plates;
the container openings, pitches, 550 ml extra height and total insertion
depths are preserved. Modules of different versions will not have flush
outer borders. No side-to-side cabinet connector is included.

## Files in each part folder

- `3dpr.scad` / `.stl`: the entire kit laid flat, with all required quantities.
  It is an overview and may exceed one bed.
- `front.scad` / `.stl`: print one front plate with the catch lip.
- `back.scad` / `.stl`: print one rear plate without the catch lip.
- `face_plate.scad` / `.stl`: compatibility alias of the front plate only.
  Do not print this twice for a full unit.
- `rail.scad` / `.stl`: one repeatable shelf/top/top-corner rail.
- `bottom_rail.scad` / `.stl`: one reinforced bottom-row rail.
- `rails_plate_01.scad` / `.stl`, etc.: bed-sized batches containing the exact
  quantities of regular and bottom rails. Print each batch once. The A1 mini
  1 x 4 requires two batches; the other current full units require one.
- `assembled.scad` / `.stl`: assembled reference, not a one-piece print.
- `assembly.json`: bill of materials and view names; `printing.txt`: assembly
  and print notes. These are generated by the normal SCAD output stage.

For a `column x row` grid, print one front and one back, `(column + 1) * (row + 1)`
regular rails and `column + 1` bottom rails. Use two screws and two nuts per
rail. A 2 x 7 P1P unit therefore uses 24 regular rails, 3 bottom rails,
54 screws and 54 nuts. A 4 x 12 unit uses 65 regular rails, 5 bottom rails,
140 screws and 140 nuts. Hardware costs are excluded from comparison prices.

## Test first

All tests are normal population records under `parts/`, with `ai_version_4_style`:

1. The 100 ml `_joint_fit` folder contains two small identical mounting coupons
   and a 30 mm rail. Print the three-piece `3dpr.stl` kit. Check side nut entry,
   countersunk seating and both alignment keys using two M3 x 16 screws.
2. `_face_fit` folders for both 100 ml and 550 ml contain one complete 1 x 1
   front plate with the real opening and both catch slopes in `3dpr.stl`.
   The same folder also has `back.stl` for an optional rear-opening trial.
   These samples contain no pots in their cost estimate.
3. Print a complete 1 x 1 unit to test tray seating through the clear rear,
   running fit, rail deflection and screw tightening before a large unit.

Face-fit files are one plate only. Their BOM deliberately differs from the
full-unit formula above. The short joint coupon is a hardware trial, not a
tray-clearance or full-length bending test.

## Source and verification

Keep `working_oomp_populate -> working_oomp -> working_scad`.
Population owns sizes, containers and all adjustable v4 settings. Version 4
reads the frozen v3 dimensional mapping and basic opening parameters, but has
its own component bodies and complete cutout routine. All plate openings,
guides, keys, countersinks, rail reliefs, sockets and nut cuts are in
`get_container_cutout_ai_version_4()` with explicit multiline dictionaries.
Positive plate/rail bodies and layouts stay in `working_scad.py`.

Run `python action_validate_ai_version_4.py` for the full pipeline, exports,
mesh checks, previews and navigation. `--skip-generation` is only for checking
outputs immediately after a complete pipeline run. Current validation passes
81 exported views and eleven assembly/sample records: watertightness,
connected printable parts, bed placement/limits, front alias identity, key fit,
nominal nut insertion samples, socket/roof/wall retention, front guide ramps,
absence of the rear catch and an exact geometry comparison of the removed lip,
localized rail bridging, no plate/rail interference and exact kit/batch BOM
volumes. It also checks all three release locks. There are 38 passing geometry
unit tests; mesh checks do not establish printing, physical fit or strength.
The six comparison tests and all three release checks also pass after the
final catalogue refresh; all 48 printed measurement fingerprints are current.

Run `python action_prepare_drawer_comparison.py` after geometry changes.
Comparison costs use the assembled v4 model, not a single rail or plate;
fit samples use their actual printed contents and no pots. The active catalogue
has 48 printed and 15 purchased records; 52 complete units are shown by default
and eleven samples are hidden. No clearance-fit variants are added.
